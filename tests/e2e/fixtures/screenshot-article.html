City approves new bike-lane network

The city council voted on Tuesday evening to approve a sweeping plan that will add forty kilometres of protected bike lanes across the downtown core and the eastern neighbourhoods. Supporters called it the most significant transport decision in a generation.

Construction is expected to begin in the spring and to take roughly two years, with the busiest corridors being completed first. Engineers said the work will be phased so that no more than three major streets are affected at the same time.

Funding for the project comes largely from a regional transport grant, supplemented by a smaller contribution from the municipal capital budget. Council staff estimate that the network will cost less than the resurfacing of a single highway interchange.

Opponents raised concerns about lost parking and delivery access for small businesses. In response, the plan includes short loading zones on every block and a review after the first twelve months of operation.

Residents can submit comments on the detailed designs until the end of the month, either online or at one of three open houses scheduled in local libraries. The council will publish a summary of the feedback before final drawings are approved.

Cycling groups said they were delighted, while the chamber of commerce urged the city to keep a close eye on traffic patterns during construction and to communicate clearly about detours.
